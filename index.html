Chen Chang, Ph.D.

CRISPR Technology Development · Functional Genomics · Cell Engineering

I am a postdoctoral researcher at UT Southwestern Medical Center specializing in CRISPR technology development. My research spans genome editing, functional genomics, single-cell Perturb-seq, and CAR T-cell engineering, with a focus on developing technologies to study and manipulate complex biological systems.

My recent work centers on PAIR-seq (Parallel Activation and Interference CRISPR with Sequencing), a combinatorial CRISPR platform I developed to systematically investigate genetic interactions through simultaneous gene activation and repression. By integrating genetic perturbations with single-cell genomics, I seek to understand how genes interact to regulate cellular responses and identify new strategies for precision genome engineering.

Looking ahead, I am interested in developing next-generation genetic perturbation and cell-tracing technologies to investigate how genetic programs and environmental signals interact to shape cell identity, function, and fate.

Research

Developing new CRISPR technologies to understand biology and enable precise cellular engineering.

01

Bidirectional CRISPR Perturbation

Developed PAIR, a bidirectional CRISPR platform enabling simultaneous gene activation and repression, and PAIR-seq for systematic investigation of genetic interactions through pooled combinatorial screening.

02

Precision Genome Editing

Applied PAIR-seq to systematically investigate genetic interactions between DNA repair pathways, particularly HDR vs. NHEJ. Identified bidirectional perturbation combinations that shift DNA repair toward HDR, improving precise genome editing efficiency.

03

Rational Cell Engineering

Developed CRISPR-based strategies to enhance precise genome editing in human primary T cells by modulating DNA repair pathways, enabling more efficient non-viral CAR T-cell engineering.
